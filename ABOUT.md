# Unseen — what it is, in my words

I'm building Unseen because I've spent enough years on dating apps to know exactly what they're doing wrong. They optimize for the swipe. I'm optimizing for what comes after.

## The idea

Most dating apps work like this: see a photo and a profile, judge in two seconds, swipe, match, instant chat that often goes nowhere — and the whole identity is revealed up front, so you've already decided before you've spoken a word.

Unseen flips that. You match on photos only — no bios, no jobs, no prompts. You can't talk yet. The chat unlocks about a day later, and even then, you don't know which of the people you swiped on actually matched with you. You can only learn about that person by talking to them. There's no "now you see the full profile" moment. The only way through is conversation.

## What makes it different

**Bias by subtraction.** Bios filter on class. Prompts filter on cleverness. Job titles filter on income. Removing them doesn't remove bias entirely — you still react to the photo — but it strips out the layer that pretends to be about "compatibility" and is actually about social signal sorting.

**Curiosity built in.** The 24-hour delay sounds like friction; it is friction. It's the kind that makes the chat actually mean something when it opens. Matches show up as anonymous labels (BlueOtter42), not faces, so you can't pre-decide who you want to talk to based on who they "look like."

**Conversation as the only signal.** When the only window into someone is the words they choose, the people who actually want to talk show up. The people who want an instant hookup queue go elsewhere. That's the point, not a side effect.

## How it works, in five steps

1. Sign up with an email.
2. Set your filters — gender preference, age relation, languages.
3. Upload photos.
4. Swipe — just photos, nothing else.
5. When two of you both swipe right, you have a match. About a day later the chat opens. You start a conversation, plan a date when you want to, and meet to find out who's behind the words.

Safety lives inside the chat: you plan dates with an emergency contact attached, the place gets a Google Maps link, conversations stay private, and you can unmatch or report any time.

## What I took from years of dating

That instant access doesn't make connection more likely; it makes it less likely. That the photos and bios people put up aren't honest signals — they're performances. That the way most apps are built rewards the worst behavior, because the worst behavior generates the most engagement, and engagement is what these apps actually optimize for.

I wanted to build something where the design itself protected the kind of slow, curious, real connection most dating apps have given up on. Where the friction was the feature, not the price you pay. Where the experience selected for the kind of people I actually wanted to meet.

That's Unseen.
